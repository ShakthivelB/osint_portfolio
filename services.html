Understand YourDigital Exposure

Public information can reveal more than people and organizations expect. I provide scoped Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT) assessments to help you understand what is publicly discoverable, evaluate relevant exposure risks, and decide what to address first.

Assessments use lawful public sources and explicitly authorized assets. Findings reflect what could be verified during the agreed assessment period; they cannot guarantee that every possible exposure has been found.

01 / PERSONAL

Personal Digital Footprint Assessment

A structured review of information about a consenting client that can be found through public websites, search engines, public social profiles, and other appropriate open sources.

What the assessment can include

  • Search results and publicly indexed pages relevant to you.
  • Public professional and social profiles you identify or authorize for review.
  • Public usernames, biographies, posts, profile links, mentions, and documents that may expose unnecessary personal information.
  • Relevant, publicly discoverable contact details and potential privacy or impersonation concerns.
  • Recommendations for privacy settings, account protection, and requesting removals where a site provides a suitable process.

What you receive

A readable footprint summary, a source register with links and observation dates where safe to share, an explanation of exposure and confidence, and a prioritized action plan.

Suitable for: professionals, job seekers, creators, founders, and people reviewing their own online presence.

Scope: You must confirm the identity or accounts and purpose. This does not access private accounts, bypass access controls, or guarantee discovery or removal of all content.

03 / ACCOUNT PROTECTION

Breach & Credential Exposure Check

A check for reports associated with client-provided email addresses or organization-owned accounts, using reputable breach-notification sources and legitimate exposure-intelligence services.

What the check can include

  • Checks against legitimate breach-notification sources that permit the intended use.
  • Relevant reported incidents and data categories described by the source, where available.
  • Source and observation date, with a distinction between source-reported incidents and unverified indicators.
  • Recommendations for unique passwords, password managers, multi-factor authentication, session review, and account recovery checks.

What you receive

An exposure summary for the agreed identifiers, source and date for relevant results, a confidence note, and a prioritized account-protection checklist.

Suitable for: people checking their own email addresses and organizations reviewing organization-owned accounts.

Privacy: Never send passwords. This service does not accept passwords or collect or redistribute stolen credential databases. No known match does not prove an account was never exposed; a breach listing alone does not prove current compromise.

04 / PUBLIC PROFILES

Public Social Profile & Username Review

A review of public profiles associated with usernames or accounts supplied by the client, using public platform pages and appropriate search methods.

What the review can include

  • Searching for exact username matches on public platforms and websites.
  • Comparing public profile details to estimate whether a result is relevant.
  • Reviewing public biographies, posts, links, and account descriptions.
  • Identifying possible duplicate, abandoned, misleading, or impersonating profiles.
  • Recommendations for privacy settings and reporting suspected impersonation.

What you receive

A list of relevant public URLs with context, match rationale, a confidence rating with explanation, and practical next steps.

Suitable for: clients reviewing their own presence, creators, professionals, and businesses protecting official brand accounts.

Limitations: A matching username, image, or biography does not prove that accounts belong to the same person. This review does not access private accounts, evade platform controls, or seek non-public personal information.

02 / ORGANIZATIONS

Domain & External Exposure Review

A scoped review of the public-facing footprint of a domain or organization-owned internet asset, to help an authorized owner understand what appears visible from the outside.

What the review can include

  • Passive subdomain discovery using appropriate public sources.
  • DNS records, certificate-transparency observations, discovered hostnames, and publicly reachable web services.
  • Review of accessible files or pages that may disclose unnecessary information.
  • Basic checks of agreed, authorized public-facing assets for observable exposure indicators.
  • Validation and deduplication to reduce noise and false positives.

What you receive

An external asset inventory, validated exposure observations with source and date where applicable, risk prioritization, and practical remediation guidance for your technical team.

Suitable for: small businesses, startups, website owners, and organizations seeking an initial external exposure review.

Authorization: Written authorization and an agreed scope are required before active checks. Exploitation, password attacks, denial-of-service testing, and intrusive scanning are excluded unless separately and explicitly agreed. This is not a full penetration test.

05 / ORGANIZATIONS

Business & Brand OSINT Assessment

A public-source assessment of an organization’s visible online presence and information-exposure indicators, to help it understand what an outside observer can learn from its public footprint.

What the assessment can include

  • Public company websites, domains, and official social profiles.
  • Publicly documented technology and infrastructure clues.
  • Indexed documents and metadata that may reveal unnecessary business information.
  • Potential impersonation, typosquatting, or brand-abuse indicators visible through public sources.
  • Relevant publicly reported incidents or exposure signals, with confirmed observations separated from hypotheses.

What you receive

A public footprint summary, evidence-linked exposure register with observation dates, risk context, and prioritized recommendations for technical, security, or communications teams.

Suitable for: small businesses, startups, founders, and teams seeking an initial view of their public information footprint.

Limitations: This is an OSINT assessment, not a legal investigation or full penetration test, and it cannot guarantee all risks will be found. Active technical checks include authorized assets only.

06 / RESEARCH

Public-Source Research Brief

Focused research answering a clearly defined question using lawful, publicly accessible, and verifiable sources.

Potential use cases

  • Verify publicly documented professional or business claims.
  • Research a company, product, or brand’s public footprint.
  • Map publicly documented organizational relationships.
  • Review public indicators of impersonation or fraud.
  • Compile a sourced timeline of public statements, incidents, or disclosures.
  • Support a business decision with traceable public information.

What you receive

An executive summary, an evidence table with sources, dates, and confidence, a timeline or relationship summary when relevant, and clear limitations for gaps or unverified claims.

Suitable for: businesses, researchers, journalists, and clients with a legitimate, clearly scoped public-source research need.

Boundaries: No doxxing, stalking, covert tracking, private-account access, social engineering, or dossiers intended to harass, intimidate, or target private individuals. Sensitive personal information is avoided unless necessary, lawful, and explicitly in scope.

A clear process

How an engagement works

  1. ScopeAgree on the goal, identity or asset ownership, boundaries, timeline, and deliverables before work begins.
  2. DiscoverReview appropriate public sources and authorized tools while avoiding unnecessary data collection.
  3. ValidateCross-check findings where possible and record sources, dates, confidence, and potential false positives.
  4. ReportReceive a clear summary, risk context, evidence, prioritized recommendations, and limitations.
  5. Follow upIf agreed, review remediation progress or repeat a scoped check later.

Evidence you can act on

Where relevant to the service and agreed scope, reports include a plain-language summary, scope and methodology, sources and observation dates, validation and confidence, risk context, prioritized actions, and limitations.

Reports are shared only with the authorized client contact. Sensitive client findings are not published in the portfolio without explicit written permission. Public-source findings are time-bound observations; websites and exposures can change after an assessment.
